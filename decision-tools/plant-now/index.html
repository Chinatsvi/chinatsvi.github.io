Crop Information

How will you grow this crop?

Why farming method matters

Rain-fed farming depends mainly on rainfall. If rainfall starts late or stops soon after planting, young plants may struggle. Irrigated farmers have more control over water, but they still need to consider temperature and other growing conditions.

Is your land ready for planting?

Why land preparation matters

Good seed planted into poorly prepared land can still give poor results. Preparing the field before planting helps the crop establish properly. This does not mean perfect preparation is required in every farming system, but basic preparation is important.

What "ready" typically means:
  • Land is prepared (tilled, beds formed, etc.)
  • Weeds are under control
  • Soil is workable (not too wet, not too hard)
  • Drainage is acceptable
  • Required inputs are available or can be obtained
  • Planting area is accessible

How is the soil moisture?

Why soil moisture matters

Seed needs suitable moisture to start growing. Very dry soil can prevent good germination, while waterlogged soil can reduce oxygen around roots and damage young plants. Different crops and soil types have different moisture requirements.

How to check soil moisture:
  • Take a handful of soil from planting depth (5-10 cm deep)
  • Squeeze it in your hand
  • Moist: Soil forms a ball but breaks apart easily
  • Dry: Soil falls apart and does not form a ball
  • Very wet: Water drips out when squeezed, soil is muddy
  • Check several spots in the field
  • Consider recent rainfall and weather

Do you have enough water for this crop?

Why water availability matters

Rainfall may be enough today but the crop may need water for several weeks after planting. Before planting, think about what will happen if rainfall becomes unreliable. Young plants are especially sensitive to water stress.

Things to consider:
  • How long will your water source last?
  • What happens if rainfall is delayed?
  • Do you have a backup water plan?
  • Is your water source reliable during dry periods?
  • Have you checked your water source recently?

Business Planning Check

Why business planning matters

Growing a crop is only part of farming as a business. You also need to consider costs, potential income, and where you will sell your harvest. A crop can produce a good harvest and still lose money if costs are too high or the selling price is poor.

Before planting, consider:
  • Cost of seeds/seedlings
  • Fertilizer and soil amendments
  • Labor costs (planting, weeding, harvesting)
  • Water costs (if irrigated)
  • Pest and disease control
  • Equipment and tools
  • Transport and packaging
  • Expected yield and selling price
