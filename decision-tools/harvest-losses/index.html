    Practical Post-Harvest Guide

    How to Eliminate Food Loss & Protect Your Hard-Earned Crop

    A farmer's effort does not end at harvesting. Learn the science and practical tricks of grain drying, onion curing, fruit crating, and weevil defense.

    1. The Simple Salt Jar Test for Grain Moisture

    You don't need an expensive electric moisture meter to test if your maize or beans are dry enough for storage! Take a clean, dry glass jar, fill it 70% with grain, add 2 tablespoons of dry table salt, and shake vigorously for 1 minute. Let it settle for 10 minutes. If salt sticks to the glass walls, the grain is still damp (> 14% moisture) and will rot. If the salt falls cleanly to the bottom, the grain is safe (< 13% moisture) to bag!

    2. How to Cure Onions and Potatoes

    Never pack fresh onions or potatoes into sacks right after pulling from the field. Curing means spreading them in a single layer in a shady, warm, well-ventilated barn for 10 to 14 days. During curing, onion necks shrivel tightly shut (sealing out rot bacteria) and potato skins thicken and toughen, doubling their shelf life.

    3. The Secret of PICS Hermetic Bags

    Purdue Improved Crop Storage (PICS) bags contain two inner airtight high-density plastic liners inside an outer woven sack. When grain is sealed at safe moisture, insects breathe up the tiny trapped oxygen within 24-48 hours and die naturally. You get 100% insect-free grain without buying chemical powders.

    4. Zero-Energy Cool Chambers (ZECC)

    A ZECC is a low-cost, electricity-free cooler built using a double-brick wall with wet river sand packed in the gap. Evaporation of water from the sand cools the inner room by 10°C to 15°C below outside ambient heat and maintains 90% humidity, keeping tomatoes, spinach, and mangoes fresh and crisp for 10-14 days.

    Common Questions

    Post-Harvest Loss FAQ

    What causes Aflatoxin in stored maize and groundnuts?

    Aflatoxin is a poisonous chemical toxin produced by the soil fungus Aspergillus flavus. It happens when crops are stressed by drought near harvest, dried directly on bare dirt, or bagged while still wet (> 13.5% moisture). Aflatoxin causes liver cancer and stunting in children. Prevent it by drying crops on plastic tarpaulins and ensuring grain is bone dry before bagging.

    Why do bottom tomatoes in wooden crates or sacks squash into liquid?

    Soft perishable fruit cannot bear excessive weight. When crates are stacked more than 5 high or packed in flexible sacks, the weight of the upper 40kg pushes down onto the bottom layer, crushing cell walls. Once fruit is punctured or bruised, bacterial soft rot spreads to the entire box within 36 hours. Always use rigid stackable plastic crates.

    How do I stop rats from eating grain in traditional wooden granaries?

    Install simple rodent baffle guards! Cut circles or cones out of old metal iron sheets or opened metal tin cans and nail them upside down (like a funnel pointing downwards) around every wooden support pole of your granary at least 1 metre above the ground. Rats and mice cannot climb past the smooth downwards metal lip.
